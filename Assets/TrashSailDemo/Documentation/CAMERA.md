# Camera and synthetic-demo vision

`DemoCameraPerception` adapts the rendered-camera technique of installed MARUS `Marus.Sensors.CameraSensor`. That upstream component exposes a double-buffered `Data` array but no capture pose, sequence or completion event; `SensorSampler` also sets its `hasData` before GPU completion. The demo therefore owns a small render adapter without modifying upstream sources. The configured HDRP camera renders to an ARGB32 texture. Begin-render captures pose/time, end-render schedules asynchronous GPU readback, and only its successful callback produces a frame. There is at most one GPU request and one latency slot. A reset generation invalidates old callbacks.

Wire `Config`, `SensorCamera` and optionally `OwnStateProvider` to the vessel's `DemoOwnState`. Without own-state wiring the adapter explicitly reports ideal camera pose. With it, the camera mounting calibration is transformed through estimated vessel pose/attitude; estimated water level is supplied by the same state interface. Default image size is 320×192 and requested rate 10 simulation Hz. Actual processed rate and inference milliseconds are exposed. GPU completion or rendering load can reduce achieved rate. Configured dropout discards captures; latency delays delivery; FreezeFeed leaves the last completed image visibly stale. No queue of old inference tasks accumulates.

The default detector is **a controlled-colour synthetic-demo baseline**, not trained real-rubbish recognition. It finds connected red/orange RGB components, estimates compact bounding rectangles and assigns generic `rubbish` only when its saturation-derived heuristic score reaches 0.72. Muted candidates remain `unknown`. Scores are not calibrated probabilities. It cannot distinguish real bottles/cans/packaging reliably or generalize to arbitrary lighting/backgrounds. No model weights, external inference package or model licence is involved. Geometry and materials are original demo assets; upstream MARUS remains Apache-2.0.

Operational detection reads only `CameraFrame.Pixels` and image dimensions. There are no object searches, names, tags, annotation boxes, object positions or segmentation labels in the detector/tracker. The camera excludes hull layer 8 and underwater-actor layer 9; the calm opaque water is the primary underwater-visibility approximation. A submerged fish produces no camera detection. Disabling annotation output or renaming scenario objects does not alter pixel processing.

Floating-object range comes from the lowest visible component band (approximate water-contact point), not the rectangle centre. A calibrated pinhole ray intersects the estimated water plane. Contact touching the lower image crop, a near-horizontal ray, a camera below the water, or a range beyond 35 m is invalid. Position uncertainty grows with distance and grazing angle. This approximation is biased for objects protruding above water and cannot range submerged objects. The operational tracker associates estimated positions, creates its own integer IDs, retains missed contacts for configured memory, and increases uncertainty rather than refreshing stale timestamps.

The retained RGB buffer uses bottom-left pixel origin. Preview texture and overlay boxes are generated from the same completed sequence; GUI mapping explicitly converts bottom-left pixels to top-left panel coordinates. HDRP automatic target-texture orientation is used. `FlipReadbackY` is an explicit calibration override for a graphics backend with reversed raw readback, and must be confirmed by the integrated rendered projection check. No image orientation is guessed from object truth during operation.

Focused Edit Mode tests in `CameraTests.cs` cover RGB-only segmentation, unknown scores, attitude-aware water projection, horizon/crop rejection, track identity/expiry/reset, and overlay coordinates. These fixtures do not by themselves prove GPU rendering. The lead's rendered acceptance run must show the actual onboard RGB frame, matching boxes and collection loop, and record camera readback errors/latency/rate.

For real hardware, replace the frame source with timestamped calibrated RGB imagery, provide synchronized estimated vessel attitude and water plane, then train and evaluate a detector on representative rubbish, water/glare, fouling and weather images. Measure class precision/recall and probability calibration separately. Keep range uncertainty and stale-frame interlocks even after replacing this baseline.
